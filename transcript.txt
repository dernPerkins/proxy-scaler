● The plan is written. Summary of the shape:

  The Back Library is the template — content-addressed sha256, client owns the
  bytes, server keeps a cache filled by a GET-probe-then-POST sync.
  custom_images.rs and customs.py are near-copies of back_images.rs and
  backs.py. That gives you "upload only when needed" for free and one identical
  code path for Local and Remote.

  The part that isn't a copy is identity. Backs specifically refused upscaling
  to avoid "a synthetic Scryfall identity, registry rows that no decklist can 
  ever match". You want upscaling, so that has to be confronted: rows carry
  either a scryfall_id or a custom_hash (never both, enforced by CHECK),
  canonical string custom:<sha256>. That's a migration-008 table rebuild on
  generation_tasks and generated_images, since both columns are NOT NULL and
  SQLite can't relax that in place. Migration 006 already did this kind of
  rebuild, so there's a pattern to follow — but it's the riskiest step in the
  plan.

  One trap worth flagging before you read further. preferred_dpi is a deliberate
  hard filter in _pick_dpi_variant, and use_originals is deliberately
  exclusive. Both rules are correct for Scryfall cards and both silently break
  custom cards: a user with Preferred DPI 1200 who hasn't upscaled their custom
  art would get a hole in the sheet rather than the image they explicitly
  supplied. The plan resolves this with a third sentinel (model = 
  "custom_source") plus two narrow, commented exemptions in pdf_layout.py.
  That's the single design decision I'd want you to sanity-check, since it's
  where a future reader is most likely to "clean up" the asymmetry and regress
  it.

  Smaller things the plan pins down: cover-cropping happens server-side at store
  time (so upscale/PDF/ZIP all need no special case, and your uncropped
  original survives on the client); postprocess.clean_original_png is explicitly
  guarded off for custom images since its fixups are Scryfall-specific; custom
  filenames omit -SET-COLLECTOR rather than faking them, because fake values
  would collide across every custom card in match_quantities; and the dropzone
  is deliberately not gated on server availability, unlike the resolve-gated
  decklist import.

     ---

     The one genuinely hard design point

     Backs avoided upscaling to avoid "a synthetic Scryfall identity, registry 
     rows
     that no decklist can ever match" (proxy_scaler/backs.py:10-18). We're
     taking
     that on deliberately, so the identity has to be explicit rather than a fake
     UUID.

     A Custom Image's identity is its sha256 content hash. Server-side, rows
     carry
     either a scryfall_id or a custom_hash, never both, never neither. The
     canonical string form is custom:<sha256>, used wherever an identity key is
     built
     (db, mergeCardStatus.cardIdentity, project_store.entry_dedup_key).

     The preferred_dpi trap

     _pick_dpi_variant (pdf_layout.py:599) makes preferred_dpi a hard filter —
     a face without that DPI is dropped and reported in missing_at_dpi, never
     substituted. That rule is right for Scryfall cards (it stops silent
     resolution
     mixing between generated variants of the same card). It is wrong for a
     custom
     card the user never upscaled: their supplied source is the only image that
     exists,
     so filtering it out prints a hole instead of the card they explicitly
     provided.

     Likewise use_originals is exclusive by design (match_quantities,
     pdf_layout.py:690)
     — registering un-upscaled customs under the ORIGINAL_MODEL sentinel would
     force
     users to tick "Use 300 DPI originals" to print them, which would then blank
     every
     upscaled Scryfall card on the sheet.

     Resolution: un-upscaled customs register under a third sentinel,
     model = "custom_source" (dpi = true print DPI computed from pixel dims at
     63×88, the same measure back_images.rs::dpi_at_card_size already uses). Two
     narrow rules in pdf_layout.py:

     1. match_quantities' (item.model == ORIGINAL_MODEL) == use_originals filter
        gains a third arm: custom_source rows are always eligible, either way.
     2. _pick_dpi_variant: for a face-group whose items are custom,
        preferred_dpi
        degrades from hard filter to preference — fall back to highest-DPI
        available
        rather than returning (None, True).

     Both need a comment explaining why the asymmetry exists, in the style of
     the
     surrounding code.
